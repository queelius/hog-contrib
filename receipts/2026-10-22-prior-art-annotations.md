## HoG 868 (sat-c6-n7)

This graph is C6-free and C6-saturated: adding any missing edge creates a 6-cycle. With 10 edges it realizes sat(7,C6)=10. This value, with this graph (Y37), was already found by exhaustive computer search in A. Jambulapati and R. Faudree, "A Collection of Results on Saturation Numbers" (University of Memphis technical report, 2014), https://digitalcommons.memphis.edu/speccoll-faudreerj/27, which lists sat(n,C6)=n+3 for n=6,...,9. A. Towell, "Graph Saturation Numbers for Small Subgraphs: Exact Computations via SAT", [Zenodo v3](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21350167), reproduces it by SAT; the byte-exact graph6 witness was independently checked. House of Graphs witness ledger, record sat-c6-n7, doi:10.5281/zenodo.23550687. Keywords: C6-saturated, saturation number, extremal graph.

## HoG 57145 (sat-c6-n8)

Unique (up to isomorphism) minimum C6-saturated graph on 8 vertices: C6-free, and adding any missing edge creates a 6-cycle, so it realizes sat(8,C6)=11. 8 vertices, 11 edges, degrees (4,4,3,3,3,2,2,1), 2 triangles, not bipartite. The value is not new: it appears with this graph (Y38) in A. Jambulapati and R. Faudree, "A Collection of Results on Saturation Numbers" (Univ. of Memphis technical report, 2014), https://digitalcommons.memphis.edu/speccoll-faudreerj/27. An earlier version of this comment called it new; that claim is withdrawn. Added here is uniqueness: complete isomorph-free enumeration of all 8-vertex graphs finds no C6-saturated graph with at most 10 edges and exactly one with 11; SAT is UNSAT at 10 edges. A. Towell, "Graph Saturation Numbers for Small Subgraphs: Exact Computations via SAT", [Zenodo v3](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21350167), Sec. 2.3. House of Graphs witness ledger, record sat-c6-n8, doi:10.5281/zenodo.23550687.

## HoG 57172 (sat-c5-n8)

A minimum C5-saturated graph on 8 vertices: no 5-cycle, while adding any missing edge creates one, so it realizes sat(8,C5)=10. 10 edges; degrees (4,4,3,3,3,1,1,1); 3 triangles; connected, planar, non-bipartite. The value appears in A. Jambulapati and R. Faudree, "A Collection of Results on Saturation Numbers" (Univ. of Memphis technical report, 2014), https://digitalcommons.memphis.edu/speccoll-faudreerj/27, with a different minimum graph (K4 with a pendant edge at each vertex), so n=8 has at least two minima. Y.-C. Chen's formula for sat(n,C5) is proved for n >= 11 and does not cover n=8; an earlier version of this comment wrongly cited it as agreeing. A. Towell, "Graph Saturation Numbers for Small Subgraphs: Exact Computations via SAT", [Zenodo v3](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21350167), reproduces the value by SAT. House of Graphs witness ledger, record sat-c5-n8, doi:10.5281/zenodo.23550687.

## HoG 484 (sat-p4-n6)

This graph realizes sat(6,P_4)=3, the minimum number of edges in a P_4-saturated graph on 6 vertices. The matching 3K_2 has no P_4, but adding any non-edge creates one. The value is a case of the theorem of L. Kászonyi and Zs. Tuza (J. Graph Theory 10 (1986), doi:10.1002/jgt.3190100209): sat(n,P_4) = n/2 for even n and (n+3)/2 for odd n, as restated in A. Dudek and A. P. Wojda, Opuscula Math. 24 (2004), Theorem 2. So the function is non-monotone: sat(6) = 3, sat(7) = 5, sat(8) = 4. A. Towell, "Graph Saturation Numbers for Small Subgraphs: Exact Computations via SAT", [Zenodo v3](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21350167), reproduces these values by SAT. House of Graphs witness ledger, record sat-p4-n6, doi:10.5281/zenodo.23550687.

## HoG 56649 (sat-p4-n7)

This graph realizes sat(7,P_4)=5, the minimum number of edges in a P_4-saturated graph on 7 vertices. It consists of a star K_{1,4} plus a disjoint edge; it has no P_4, but adding any non-edge creates one. The value is a case of the theorem of L. Kászonyi and Zs. Tuza (J. Graph Theory 10 (1986), doi:10.1002/jgt.3190100209), sat(n,P_4) = (n+3)/2 for odd n. Its restatement in A. Dudek and A. P. Wojda, Opuscula Math. 24 (2004), Theorem 2, lists (k-1)K_2 ∪ K_3 as the only minimum graph for n = 2k+1. This graph is a second minimum at n = 7, and K_{1,4} ∪ (k-2)K_2 is one at every odd n >= 5, so the family as restated is incomplete. A. Towell, "Graph Saturation Numbers for Small Subgraphs: Exact Computations via SAT", [Zenodo v3](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21350167). House of Graphs witness ledger, record sat-p4-n7, doi:10.5281/zenodo.23550687. Keywords: P_4-saturated, graph saturation, star plus matching, extremal.

## HoG 57173 (sat-p5-n8)

A minimum P5-saturated graph on 8 vertices: it contains no path on 5 vertices as a subgraph, while adding any missing edge creates one, so it realizes sat(8,P5)=6. It is the disjoint union of a 6-vertex double star and K2, with 6 edges and degree sequence (3,3,1,1,1,1,1,1). The value is a case of the theorem of L. Kászonyi and Zs. Tuza (J. Graph Theory 10 (1986), doi:10.1002/jgt.3190100209), sat(n,P5) = ⌈(5n-4)/6⌉ for n >= 5, as restated in P. Balister and A. Dogan, J. Graph Theory 89 (2018), Theorem 4. It illustrates the plateau sat(7,P5) = sat(8,P5) = 6. A. Towell, "Graph Saturation Numbers for Small Subgraphs: Exact Computations via SAT", [Zenodo v3](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21350167), reproduces the value by SAT; the byte-exact graph6 witness was independently checked. House of Graphs witness ledger, record sat-p5-n8, doi:10.5281/zenodo.23550687. Keywords: P5-saturated, path saturation, forest, double star.
